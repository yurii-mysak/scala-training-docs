# Behavioural & Staff Scope

> **Priority:** Required
> **Est. time:** 15 min (this file); ~10 h for the section
> **Track:** Both
> **HelloInterview:** none

The behavioural round is 45 minutes out of a loop that runs six-plus hours, and it is the round most
likely to cost the level.

---

## 1 · The risk, stated bluntly

**The experience reads Senior.** Thirteen years, genuine breadth — JVM/Scala/Akka, .NET, Node,
AWS/Terraform, Lua — and long tenures that show follow-through. All of it good. All of it described
as **systems personally built**.

That is a T5 profile. At T6 the protagonist of the story is the organisation, and the candidate is
the person who changed its direction.

Three mechanisms turn that framing problem into an actual downlevel:

| Mechanism | Effect |
|-----------|--------|
| Level is set by a **hiring committee** reading written interviewer notes | Your stories are re-told by someone who was not in the room. Scope that was implied does not survive transcription. |
| Lyft runs a **lagging-promotion** model | Levelling is on evidence of already operating at Staff, not on potential. "Ready to grow into it" is a Senior answer. |
| The behavioural round is the **only** round carrying organisational evidence | Technical rounds prove you can build. Nothing else can prove you change how a group of teams works. |

Add the format: **breadth over depth** — 6-9 questions in 45 minutes, many short stories rather than
three long ones, with two to four follow-ups on every answer. A candidate who prepared three deep
stories covers a third of the round and reads as having had a narrow career.

And the room: in Kyiv this round has been run by a **product manager**, so stories must land without
shared technical vocabulary; at Staff, expect a **bar raiser** whose brief is to test the level rather
than the fit.

## 2 · What this section does about it

Not "tell better stories" — a specific conversion, applied to every file:

1. **Reframe, do not inflate.** The organisational content was already there and was being left out.
   [senior-vs-staff-framing.md](senior-vs-staff-framing.md) shows the same events told at both levels,
   side by side, for seven common situations.
2. **Make the scope explicit enough to be written down.** Decision verbs instead of construction
   verbs, at least two humans with agency, an organisational consequence, a number, and a cost.
3. **Build breadth, not depth.** Twelve 90-second stories that expand to five minutes, not three long
   ones — [story-bank.md](story-bank.md), [carl-and-star.md](carl-and-star.md).
4. **Recover the numbers.** "How did you measure?" is asked verbatim.
   [metrics-for-stories.md](metrics-for-stories.md) is the retroactive excavation.
5. **Close the CV questions cheaply.** One year at LotusFlare, the missing entry, JVM to Python/Go,
   Lua, lapsed certifications, overlapping dates — [transition-narrative.md](transition-narrative.md).
6. **Rehearse under the real format.** Recording, interruption at two minutes, four consecutive
   probes — [mock-rehearsal-plan.md](mock-rehearsal-plan.md).

## Interview questions

Real reported Lyft behavioural questions, with the file that prepares each. One reported round asked
questions 1-4 consecutively as a single opening plus three follow-ups.

**1. Recent project you're proud of. What was your role? What was impact?** **[Reported at Lyft]**
Widest blast radius, not the most technically interesting project. Organisational problem, then your
decision, then the code. Role means the decisions you owned versus what your collaborators owned.
See [senior-vs-staff-framing.md](senior-vs-staff-framing.md), Slot 1 of
[story-bank.md](story-bank.md).

**2. How did you measure?** **[Reported at Lyft]**
Instrument, number, confidence — in that order. A number with no named source reads as something you
were told rather than something you owned. See [metrics-for-stories.md](metrics-for-stories.md).

**3. Tell me about failure. / Time you failed at something.** **[Reported at Lyft]**
A decision that was yours, a cost that was real, a mechanism you changed, and no rescue at the end.
At Staff the blast radius should be organisational. Slot 5.

**4. Did you convince your colleague about your solution, and how?** **[Reported at Lyft]**
The mechanism is the answer. What you built to make the disagreement checkable, what you conceded,
and what stops the argument recurring. Slot 3.

**5. What's inclusion for you?** **[Reported at Lyft]**
A practice you run plus one instance where it changed an outcome. Definitions score nothing. Slot 10.

**6. Tell me about how you handled a production bug you caused.** **[Reported at Lyft]**
Detection, blast radius, first action, comms, mitigation, fix, and the systemic change that removes
the class. Slot 2.

**7. What experience do you have with mentoring and creating systems?** **[Reported at Lyft]**
Both halves. One person with a concrete change in scope, then the mechanism that runs without you and
how many people have been through it. Slot 4.

**8. How do you manage a difficult partnership?** **[Reported at Lyft]**
Diagnose their incentives, not their behaviour. What you changed first, what you gave up, the working
agreement, and any escalation done deliberately and announced. Slot 6.

**9. Tell me some experience related to ML or Gen AI.** **[Reported at Lyft]**
Position honestly, answer from AI-dev-tools adoption, then bridge one component of their agent
platform to distributed-systems work you have actually done. See
[ai-and-genai-questions.md](ai-and-genai-questions.md).

**10. Why did you choose Lyft?** **[Reported at Lyft]**
Two specific, checkable reasons — the published multi-agent platform as a distributed-state problem,
and the honest write-up of the simulator distribution shift — closing on platform scope. See
[transition-narrative.md](transition-narrative.md) section 7.
