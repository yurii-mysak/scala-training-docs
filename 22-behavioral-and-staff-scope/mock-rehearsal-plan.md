# Mock Rehearsal Plan

> **Priority:** Recommended
> **Est. time:** 30 min to read, then ~6 h of practice
> **Track:** Both
> **HelloInterview:** Behavioral — Story Builder; Decode / Select / Deliver

Behavioural performance is a delivery skill, and delivery skills do not improve by reading. The gap
between a story that works on paper and one that works out loud is large, and it only closes with
recording.

The specific things this plan drills are the ones Lyft's format punishes: overrunning the 90-second
budget, collapsing under the third follow-up, and sounding rehearsed.

---

## 1 · Recording yourself

Non-negotiable, and the highest-leverage 20 minutes in this section. Video if possible — this round
is remote, so record in the same setup you will interview in.

**Protocol per story:**

1. Read only the bullets from the slot in [story-bank.md](story-bank.md). Never read prose.
2. Record the 90-second version cold. One take.
3. Do not re-record immediately. Move to the next story.
4. After four stories, watch all four back with a timer running.

**What to look for, in order of importance:**

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Over 2 minutes | Chronology instead of decisions | Cut to two or three decisions ([carl-and-star.md](carl-and-star.md) section 3.1) |
| Number arrives after 60 seconds | Context is too long | Move the number into the first two sentences |
| No other human named | Senior framing | [senior-vs-staff-framing.md](senior-vs-staff-framing.md) section 4.2 |
| Every story opens the same way | Memorised template | Vary entry points: number, conflict, mistake |
| Filler clusters ("so basically", "kind of", "you know") | Recall under load | Fewer bullets, not more practice |
| Voice flattens after 45 seconds | Reciting | Rehearse from bullets you have not memorised |
| No cost sentence anywhere | Missing trade-off | Add one explicitly |

**Watch on mute once.** Presence, energy and whether you look like you are remembering or performing
are all visible without audio, and this is a remote round where that is most of what comes through.

## 2 · The two lengths

Every slot needs both, and they are different artefacts, not the same story at different speeds.

### 2.1 The 90-second version

Target 85-100 seconds. Under 70 is thin; over 120 will be interrupted.

Drill: record all twelve back to back in one sitting, timing each. Twelve times 90 seconds is
18 minutes of talking, which is roughly the amount of speaking you will actually do in the round.
Fatigue in the last four is real and is exactly what happens at minute 35 of the interview.

### 2.2 The 5-minute version

Not a slower 90 seconds — the core plus expansion layers (alternatives, opposition, mechanism,
aftermath). Drill it by having the layers prompted in random order:

1. Deliver the 90-second core.
2. Draw a layer at random and add it, unprompted, in under 60 seconds.
3. Repeat until all four are used or you run dry.

Running dry is useful information: it tells you which layer is missing from the slot and needs
filling in the bank.

## 3 · Follow-up drilling

The reported Lyft round asked three consecutive follow-ups on a single opening question. Practise to
four.

### 3.1 Solo method

Write the four probes into the slot before you rehearse it, then answer them aloud in sequence
without stopping between them. The probe families are in
[carl-and-star.md](carl-and-star.md) section 6: ownership split, measurement, counterfactual,
opposition, scale, aftermath.

Rule: **never pause the recording to think.** The silence is the data. If you needed eight seconds to
answer "how big was the team", you will need eight seconds in the room.

### 3.2 Hostile-probe list

Run these against every story once. They are deliberately uncomfortable.

- What did you personally do, as opposed to the team?
- How did you measure that?
- Who disagreed with you, and what was their best argument?
- What did it cost?
- What would you do differently?
- Is it still running? Who owns it now?
- Why didn't you do that sooner?
- What's the part of this you're least proud of?

Any story where three or more of these produce nothing is not ready.

### 3.3 With a partner

If you can get one, brief them to do exactly two things: (1) interrupt at 2 minutes, every time;
(2) ask "how did you measure?" after every single answer, regardless of relevance. Those two
behaviours reproduce most of what the real round does to candidates.

Brief them not to be nice. A mock partner who lets you finish is not simulating the round.

## 5 · Self-scoring rubric

Score each recorded story out of 20. Anything under 14 goes back into the bank for rework.

| # | Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|-----------|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Length** | Over 2 min or under 60s | 100-120s | 85-100s |
| 2 | **Opening** | Context rambles | Gets there | Problem and stakes in two sentences |
| 3 | **Decisions vs chronology** | Step-by-step narration | Mixed | Two or three decisions with rejected alternatives |
| 4 | **Humans** | Only you | One other named role | Two or more, at least one disagreeing |
| 5 | **Scope** | One service, one team | Team-level | Multiple teams, or a standard others adopted |
| 6 | **Number** | None | Vague ("much faster") | Before/after with a time window |
| 7 | **Instrument** | Not mentioned | Implied | Named, with honest confidence bounds |
| 8 | **Cost** | Nothing was given up | Implied trade-off | Explicit: what was deferred, overruled or lost |
| 9 | **Learning** | Absent or a platitude | A personal lesson | A mechanism that changed and outlived you |
| 10 | **Delivery** | Recited or halting | Adequate | Conversational, varied, self-corrects naturally |

### 5.1 Round-level rubric

Score once per full mock, not per story.

| Criterion | Pass condition |
|-----------|----------------|
| Coverage | Six or more distinct slots used; no slot used twice |
| Value balance | At least one story per value |
| Follow-up survival | No answer collapsed inside four probes |
| Honesty | No number invented; every "I don't remember" followed by a bound and a source |
| Non-technical intelligibility | A non-engineer could follow every story's first sentence |
| Time | Never interrupted for length |

The last two matter specifically here: the Kyiv round has been run by a product manager, and the
round is 45 minutes with 6-9 questions.

## 6 · Three-week schedule

Assumes the bank exists in draft by the start of week 1.

| Week | Focus | Work |
|------|-------|------|
| **1** | Content | Fill all twelve slots. Run the two-hour metric recovery from [metrics-for-stories.md](metrics-for-stories.md) section 6. Run the rewrite drill from [senior-vs-staff-framing.md](senior-vs-staff-framing.md) section 5 on every slot. No rehearsal yet. |
| **2** | Delivery | Record all twelve at 90 seconds, four per sitting. Score against section 5. Rework anything under 14. Re-record the reworked ones. Drill Select speed daily, ten minutes. |
| **3** | Pressure | Follow-up drilling on all twelve to four probes. Two full 45-minute mocks with a partner, scored at round level. Rehearse the [transition-narrative.md](transition-narrative.md) answers to under 30 seconds each. |

### 6.1 Day before

- Read the coverage grid and the twelve primary numbers. Nothing else.
- Deliver three stories out loud, cold, to warm up the voice.
- Do **not** re-record, re-score or rewrite. Late changes overwrite the version you have practised
  and produce a worse delivery than the one you already had.

### 6.2 Day of

- Have the coverage grid visible but not the stories. Tick slots as you spend them.
- Have the twelve primary numbers visible. Nothing else on screen.
- Reading from notes is visible on video. The grid is a tally, not a script.

## 7 · Common rehearsal mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence |
|---------|-------------|
| Rehearsing from written prose | Produces memorised delivery, which fails **Be yourself** |
| Practising only the strongest three stories | The round asks 6-9 questions; the weak slots are where you will be caught |
| Never being interrupted in practice | Recovery from interruption is untrained, and it will happen |
| Skipping the recording because it is uncomfortable | The discomfort is the point; nobody hears their own filler otherwise |
| Rehearsing alone only | Solo practice cannot simulate an unexpected probe |
| Polishing past week 2 | Diminishing returns; spend the time on follow-up survival instead |

## Interview questions

**1. Recent project you're proud of. What was your role? What was impact? How did you measure?** **[Reported at Lyft]**
The four-part rehearsal target. Practise it as one opening plus three follow-ups delivered without a
pause between them, and time the whole exchange — it should run about three minutes total.

**2. Tell me about failure.** **[Reported at Lyft]**
Rehearse specifically for the absence of a rescue. Record it and check that the ending is the
mechanism you changed, not a recovery of the outcome. Most people add the rescue unconsciously when
speaking.

**3. Did you convince your colleague about your solution, and how?** **[Reported at Lyft]**
Drill the opposition layer: state their argument first and fairly, out loud, before yours. This is
hard to do naturally and is worth several rehearsals on its own.

**4. Can you keep that shorter?**
Practise the recovery: restart from the number and the decision in one sentence each, no apology
beyond three words. Have your mock partner interrupt at two minutes every time so this is trained
rather than improvised.

**5. What's inclusion for you?** **[Reported at Lyft]**
The story most likely to come out as a rehearsed definition. Record it and check whether you gave a
practice and an instance, or a position statement.

**6. Tell me some experience related to ML or Gen AI.** **[Reported at Lyft]**
Rehearse the honest positioning sentence until it is comfortable, because it is the part people rush.
See [ai-and-genai-questions.md](ai-and-genai-questions.md) section 2.1.

**7. Do you have another example of that?**
Drill Select: this is the moment coverage is won or lost. Given a repeat prompt, name a different
slot from a different employer in under three seconds, then deliver.
